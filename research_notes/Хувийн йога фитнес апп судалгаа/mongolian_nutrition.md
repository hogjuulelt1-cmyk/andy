**Research-method caveat (read first).** The web-search budget for this session ran out after ~20 queries, and the WebFetch tool was blocked by the network egress proxy for every domain tried (unicef.org, who.int, ncbi/pmc, mdpi.com, fao.org, ikon.mn, gogo.mn, legalinfo.mn, scholar.harvard.edu, insidemongolia.mn, cdt.amegroups.org). ## KQ1. What Mongolians actually eat today (Ulaanbaatar vs rural): meat, flour dishes, dairy, tea, sugar, salt, alcohol

### Takeaway
Mongolia remains one of the highest meat-consuming countries in the world (>100 kg/person/yr by most estimates, dominated by mutton and goat), with a diet described in the literature as predominantly meat, dairy and animal fat, very low fruit intake (about 40% of adults eat no fruit in a typical week), and salt intake roughly double the WHO limit, with salty milk tea (сүүтэй цай) a leading sodium source. Detailed 2024–2026 household menu data exist in the NSO HSES 2024 microdata but could not be extracted this session.

### Cited Findings
- Mongolia consumed over 100 kg of meat per capita in 2022 (highest-tier globally). — [Statista chart, total per-capita meat consumption](https://statista.com/chart/amp/16889/total-per-capita-meat-consumption-worldwide)
- A 2021 study cited by Tasting Table puts the average Mongolian's meat intake at nearly 309 lb (~140 kg) per year, of which over 151 lb (~68 kg) is mutton and goat — more lamb/goat per person than the next three Asian countries combined. (Secondary source; figure conflicts with the ~100 kg Statista estimate — the difference is probably carcass-weight vs retail-weight accounting, but this is my inference.) — [Tasting Table](https://www.tastingtable.com/1884231/asian-country-eats-the-most-lamb-goat-per-person)
- Goat meat (ямааны мах) per-capita consumption in Mongolia was the highest in Asia in 2024 at ~32 kg/person. — [IndexBox goat meat Asia market overview](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/goat-meat-asia-market-overview-2024-5/)
- Mongolian cuisine "predominantly consists of dairy products, meat, and animal fats", and Mongolians "traditionally consume a diet rich in animal-derived fats, often exceeding recommended limits for saturated fat intake" (dietary fat assessed by FFQ and food composition tables). — [IBCM 2025 proceedings, Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/ibcm-25/126016412)
- STEPS 2019 (n=6,654 adults 15–69): 40.2% of respondents ate fruit on 0 days per week; among fruit eaters, 33.7% ate 1 serving/day and 31.7% 2 servings/day. For vegetables, 15.0% ate none on any day of the week, 47.9% ate vegetables 7 days/week; 30.6% ate 1 serving/day and 26.4% 2 servings/day. — [WHO NCD Microdata Repository, Mongolia STEPS 2019 (catalog 836)](https://extranet.who.int/ncdsmicrodata/index.php/catalog/836)
- STEPS 2019 self-perceived salt intake: 3.6% "far too much", 20.3% "too much", 35.3% "just the right amount", 33.2% "too little" — i.e., two-thirds of adults do not think they over-consume salt despite measured intakes >11 g/day. — [WHO NCD Microdata Repository](https://extranet.who.int/ncdsmicrodata/index.php/catalog/836/variable/F3/V756?name=d8)
- WHO: a Mongolian national survey found people consuming on average more than 11 grams of salt per day (WHO limit <5 g). — [WHO WPRO, "Mongolia's pinch of salt"](https://www.who.int/westernpacific/newsroom/feature-stories/item/mongolia-s-pinch-of-salt)
- Ulaanbaatar salt study (J Hypertension abstract, 2012 — older data): overall mean salt intake in Ulaanbaatar was 12.7 g/day; salty-tea drinkers 13.9 g/d vs 12.0 g/d in non-drinkers; the main dietary salt sources were сүүтэй цай (salty milk tea), sausages, processed meats and bread (талх). — [J Hypertension abstract 1043, Ovid](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/jhypertension/fulltext/10.1097/01.hjh.0000420503.99692.39~1043-salt-intake-and-sources-of-salt-in-the-diet-in)
- Darkhan-Uul medical professionals (24-h urine, published 2023): mean sodium excretion 122.3 mmol/day ≈ 7.7 g salt/day; drinking ≥2 cups/day of salted сүүтэй цай was associated with higher odds of exceeding 5 g/day compared with ≤1 cup/day. — [Scientific Reports 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37631-x); also [PMC10307767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10307767/)
- Sugary drinks: in 2022 Mongolia was projected to sell 210 million litres of sugary drinks worth 522 billion MNT; of 16 locally produced juices/soft drinks, the highest carbohydrate content was "Fanta apple" at 12.8 g/100 ml, Pepsi 11 g, Coca-Cola and mango juice 10.2 g. — [NUM journal: Бага насны хүүхдийн чихэрлэг хүнсний хэрэглээг судалсан нь](https://journal.num.edu.mn/soc/article/view/9772)
- Among Mongolian children and adolescents, tea was the main beverage in all age/sex groups; girls aged 10–13 had the highest sugar-sweetened beverage intake. — [CAJMS, SSB and childhood obesity](https://mongoliajol.info/index.php/CAJMS/article/view/2650)
- Alcohol: WHO recorded per-capita alcohol consumption (15+) for Mongolia was 1.96 L pure alcohol in the 2004 Global Status Report (very old; recorded only — unrecorded consumption is known to be large). — [WHO 2004 report compiled by CADA](https://cada.bm/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/World_Health_Organization_2004_Report_-_Alcohol_Consumption_per_capita_by_country.pdf). A Statista series for 2005–2018 exists but values were not retrieved. — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/691309/mongolia-per-capita-alcohol-consumption)
- The NSO's Household Socio-Economic Survey 2024 microdata includes an urban food diary file (16_urb_diary), a rural 7-day food consumption file (17_rur_food_7d) and a food-away-from-home file (19_foodout), tracking purchases, own-production and gifts — the authoritative source for an urban-vs-rural menu comparison. — [NSO National Data Archive, HSES 2024](https://nada.nso.mn/en/catalog/60/data-dictionary/F21)
- Two peer-reviewed dietary assessments exist but their content could not be read this session: "Diet and Nutrition Status of Mongolian Adults" (Nutrients, May 2020) — [PMC7284332](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7284332/); "Assessment of Mongolian dietary intake for planetary and human health" (2023) — [PMC10021422](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10021422/). A Mongolian-language policy brief on correcting unbalanced diets is hosted at Harvard — [Судалгаа: Монгол хүний тэнцвэргүй хооллолтыг засахад...](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/sabri/files/sudalgaa_mongol_hunii_tencvergui_hoolloltyg_zasahad_ur_duntei_strategi_bolovsruulah_shaardlagatai.pdf)

### Inferences
- The retail-weight meat figure most useful for app calorie math is probably ~100 kg/yr ≈ 270 g/day per person (all ages), i.e., adults plausibly eat 300–400 g of mutton/beef on a typical day, most of it inside flour dishes (бууз, хуушуур, цуйван, гурилтай шөл). This is an estimate derived from the per-capita figures above.
- Because salty milk tea, bread and processed meats are the top sodium sources, the single biggest sodium lever for an app is tea salt (removing or halving salt in сүүтэй цай) and limiting sausage (хиам), not "stop adding table salt".
- The perceived-vs-actual salt gap (only ~24% think they eat too much salt while mean intake is ~11–13 g) means the app should show sodium explicitly rather than ask users to self-rate.
- Fruit is the weakest food group: 40% eat none in a typical week, so "5-a-day" messaging will fail; vegetables are a more realistic lever since ~48% already eat some every day.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 per-capita meat, flour, dairy or sugar consumption figures from the NSO (1212.mn) were retrieved; the HSES 2024 aggregate tables need a follow-up (searches in Mongolian returned only the data dictionary).
- No sourced description of a typical UB household day/week menu (how often бууз vs цуйван vs rice vs potato dishes). General knowledge (unverified this session): breakfast is often сүүтэй цай with талх/боорцог and leftovers; lunch is the main hot meal (цуйван, гурилтай шөл, банштай шөл, будаатай хуурга); бууз/хуушуур are weekend/holiday/eating-out foods; rice (будаа) and potatoes (төмс) are the main non-wheat starches; "4 vegetables" = төмс, лууван, байцаа, сонгино.
- Alcohol: no recent (2019–2025) WHO or STEPS alcohol prevalence figures retrieved (heavy episodic drinking %, litres/capita). The STEPS 2019 microdata will contain this.
- Urban vs rural dietary differences (rural: more dairy and meat, fewer vegetables; urban: more processed food, bread, soft drinks) are plausible but were not confirmed by a retrieved source.
- Dairy product consumption frequencies (сүү, тараг, ааруул, өрөм, айраг) by season were not found.

## KQ2. Nutrition facts of main dishes and dairy products

### Takeaway
No Mongolian national food-composition table, FAO/INFOODS entry, or NCPH nutrient analysis for бууз/хуушуур/цуйван was retrievable; the only numbers found are crowd-sourced Russian/Buryat analogue entries for бууз (≈190–280 kcal/100 g, 12–14 g protein, 9–12 g fat). Dairy research confirms ааруул is a concentrated protein/calcium source but gives no per-100 g figures in the snippets. All per-serving numbers below must be labelled "estimate" in the app.

### Cited Findings
- Бууз (steamed mutton dumpling) — crowd-sourced Russian entry (бузы/позы): 189.3 kcal, 11.6 g protein, 9.2 g fat, 15.6 g carbohydrate per 100 g. — [health-diet.ru user table](https://health-diet.ru/table_calorie_users/469271/)
- Alternative crowd-sourced entry for буузы/позы: 281 kcal, 14 g protein, 12 g fat, 29.5 g carbohydrate per 100 g (more dough-heavy recipe). — [Arise app, Буузы (Позы)](https://www.arise-app.com/ru/dish/Буузы-(Позы))
- A Kazan Medical Journal paper analysed the "micronutrient value and lipid profile" of the Baikal-region ethnic dish (Buryat бууз/позы), confirming academic composition data exist for this close analogue (values not in snippet). — [Kazan Medical Journal](https://kazanmedjournal.ru/kazanmedj/article/view/110913)
- Ааруул (dried curd) made from camel milk had lower water content and higher protein, fat and calcium than ааруул from other milks; a literature review found ааруул "a good source of vitamins, minerals, healthy fatty acids and proteins"; horse milk cannot form a curd when fermented, so no horse-milk ааруул exists. — [SLU thesis: Aaruul – a Mongolian dried curdled milk](https://stud.epsilon.slu.se/8197/)
- A Japanese study collected 66 samples of тараг (yoghurt), айраг (fermented mare's milk), ааруул, бяслаг (cheese) and ээзгий (dried sweet curd) and isolated 543 lactic-acid-bacteria strains, confirming these are live-culture fermented foods. — [Milk Science (J-STAGE)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/milk/55/2/55_79/_article)
- Iwate Prefectural University research "investigating the use of milk in Mongolia" reports the "general composition of Mongolia's main traditional dairy products" (table not visible in snippet). — [Iwate PU repository](https://iwate-pu.repo.nii.ac.jp/records/1932)
- A Bulgarian DSc abstract covers products made from camel milk by Mongolian nomads (хоормог etc.). — [AU Plovdiv abstracts](https://www.au-plovdiv.bg/docs/Razvitie_AS/DSc/2022/Culuunbat/Abstracts.pdf); [FAO AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/647244c3e17b74d2224f42e3)

### Inferences
- Taking 1 бууз ≈ 40–50 g cooked, the crowd-sourced values give ≈ 80–130 kcal and 5–7 g protein per бууз; a typical plate of 8–10 бууз is therefore ≈ 800–1,200 kcal and 45–65 g protein, with fat the dominant energy source when fatty mutton (хонины өөх) is used. **Estimate — must be verified against a Mongolian recipe analysis.**
- Хуушуур (deep-fried meat pastry) will be higher in fat than бууз because of frying oil absorption; цуйван (stir-fried noodles with meat) and гурилтай шөл (noodle soup) are carbohydrate-dominant with 20–30 g protein per bowl depending on meat share. **Unverified reasoning, no source.**
- Dried curds (ааруул, ээзгий) are concentrated by water removal, so their protein density per 100 g should be several times that of fresh milk — consistent with the SLU finding of higher protein/calcium in low-moisture ааруул — making them the obvious portable high-protein snack for the app. Exact g/100 g is a gap.

### Gaps
- No Mongolian food-composition table (Хүнсний найрлагын лавлах) or NCPH (НЭМҮТ) nutrient table for бууз, хуушуур, цуйван, гурилтай шөл, банштай шөл, боорцог, талх, сүүтэй цай was retrieved. General-knowledge values (unverified): ааруул ~30–45 g protein/100 g; ээзгий ~25–35 g; бяслаг ~20–25 g; аарц (fresh sour curd) ~10–15 g; тараг ~3–4 g; сүүтэй цай ~40–60 kcal per 250 ml cup plus ~0.5–1 g salt when salted. These should be flagged "estimate" or replaced with USDA analogues (e.g., USDA "cheese, cottage, dry curd" for аарц; "milk, whole" for сүү; "yogurt, whole milk" for тараг; "lamb, ground" for mutton filling).
- Sodium per serving of сүүтэй цай, гурилтай шөл and хиам (sausage) not found as published numbers.
- Fat content of Mongolian mutton (хонины мах) vs beef (үхрийн мах) vs horse (адууны мах) vs goat (ямааны мах) not retrieved; general knowledge says goat and horse are leaner than mutton (unverified here).

## KQ3. Public-health nutrition problems (obesity, hypertension, diabetes, gout, vitamin D, anemia, iodine, low fruit/veg)

### Takeaway
Adult overweight/obesity rose from 49.6% (2005) to 64.0% (2019) while hypertension fell to 23.2%; salt intake is >11 g/day; vitamin D status is among the worst documented anywhere (80% of adults <10 ng/mL in winter in a 2011–13 study, Ulaanbaatar lowest). NNS V (2017) covers anemia, iodine and child/women nutrition but its numbers could not be extracted; no Mongolia-specific gout/hyperuricemia or diabetes prevalence was found.

### Cited Findings
- STEPS trend 2005→2019 (2019 n=6,654, adults 15–69, multistage stratified sample): overweight/obesity 49.6% → 64.0%; hypertension 28.2% → 23.2%; proportion with 3–8 NCD risk factors 59.1% → 49.4%; mean number of risk factors 2.8 → 2.6. — [Iranian J Public Health, Mongolia STEPS trends](https://ijph.tums.ac.ir/index.php/ijph/article/download/27939/7987); also [KNE Publishing copy](https://publish.kne-publishing.com/index.php/ijph/article/view/13246)
- Salt: national survey mean >11 g/day; WHO supported a national salt-reduction strategy. — [WHO WPRO feature](https://www.who.int/westernpacific/newsroom/feature-stories/item/mongolia-s-pinch-of-salt); strategy paper: [Enkhtungalag et al., Cardiovascular Diagnosis and Therapy](https://cdt.amegroups.org/article/view/6401/html); process evaluation of the Malaysia & Mongolia strategies: [Public Health Nutrition 2023](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/2AE93BC6C1E5E3EEB4813B1539897261/S1368980023002781a.pdf/strengthening-national-salt-reduction-strategies-using-multiple-methods-process-evaluations-case-studies-from-malaysia-and-mongolia.pdf)
- Medical professionals in Mongolia frequently lacked knowledge of the recommended daily salt limit (study of factors related to this gap). — [PMC8067531](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8067531/)
- Vitamin D, adults (320 healthy adults 20–58 from 8 provinces, summer 2011–winter 2013 — older data): mean 25(OH)D 22.5 ng/mL in summer vs 7.7 ng/mL in winter; in winter 80.1% were <10 ng/mL, 19.5% 10–20 ng/mL, 0.3% 20–30 ng/mL, 0.0% ≥30 ng/mL. Ulaanbaatar residents had the lowest levels in both seasons, Gobi residents the highest; indoor workers lower than outdoor workers in summer; males higher than females. — [Bromage et al., PMC5083980](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5083980/)
- Drivers of low vitamin D: high latitude, increasing air pollution especially in Ulaanbaatar, no effective UVB in winter/spring, and lack of vitamin D-rich foods; a 2019 review argues for food fortification plus supplementation. — [Nutrients 2019 review, PMC6835745](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6835745/)
- A cross-sectional study of 9,595 Mongolian schoolchildren (Nutrients 2021) quantified vitamin D deficiency prevalence and determinants (numbers not in snippet). — [Nutrients 13(11):4175](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/11/4175); [Harvard-hosted PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/sites/scholar.harvard.edu/files/sabri/files/bater_2021_-_prevalence_and_determinants_of_vitamin_d_deficiency_in_9595_mongolian_schoolchildren_a_cross-sectional_study.pdf)
- NNS V (Fifth National Nutrition Survey, 2017, NCPH/MoH with UNICEF): sampled 8 districts of Ulaanbaatar and 95 soums of 21 aimags; assessed children <5, schoolchildren 6–11, pregnant women, and women and men 15–49 (includes vitamin D, anemia, iodine, overweight modules). — [UNICEF Mongolia report page](https://www.unicef.org/mongolia/reports/fifth-national-nutrition-survey-report); main report PDF: [NNS V EN](https://www.unicef.org/mongolia/media/1116/file/NNS_V_undsen_tailan_EN.pdf); thematic report: [NNS V thematic EN](https://www.unicef.org/mongolia/media/1101/file/NNS_V_Sedevchilsen_tailan_EN_PDF.pdf); Mongolian summary "Монгол Улсын хүн амын хоол тэжээлийн байдал": [UNICEF MN](https://www.unicef.org/mongolia/media/7701/file/Nutrition%20status%20of%20the%20population%20of%20Mongolia.pdf.pdf)
- Mongolia's dietary guidelines recommend ≥3 servings of vegetables and ≥2 servings of fruit per day; STEPS 2019 shows most adults fall short (see KQ1). — [FAO dietary guidelines: Mongolia](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/dietary-guidelines/regions/mongolia/en/); [WHO STEPS microdata](https://extranet.who.int/ncdsmicrodata/index.php/catalog/836)
- World Obesity Federation maintains a Mongolia "drivers" profile (diet/physical-activity drivers of obesity). — [World Obesity data, Mongolia drivers](https://data.worldobesity.org/country/mongolia-143/drivers.pdf); the Food Systems Dashboard has a Mongolia country page with diet-quality indicators. — [Food Systems Dashboard MNG](https://www.foodsystemsdashboard.org/countries/mng/fsci/printable)

### Inferences
- With ~2 in 3 adults overweight/obese and only ~1 in 4 hypertensive, the app's main population-level target is energy balance and fat/sodium reduction rather than under-nutrition — except for vitamin D and (plausibly) iron in women, where the message is "add", not "cut".
- The Ulaanbaatar-lowest vitamin D finding plus indoor-worker effect means an urban UB app user training indoors in winter should be assumed deficient by default; the app can safely recommend discussing supplementation (and sun exposure in summer) without lab tests, while leaving dosing to a clinician.

### Gaps
- NNS V numeric results (anemia in women/children, vitamin D deficiency %, iodine/goitre, women's overweight %) were not retrieved — the PDF links above are the primary source for a follow-up.
- Diabetes/raised blood glucose prevalence (STEPS 2019 and any 2023 round), raised cholesterol, and physical inactivity % were not retrieved.
- Gout/hyperuricemia: searches returned only Inner Mongolia (China) data; no Mongolia-national prevalence found. Clinically, high purine load from organ meats (дотор), broth and alcohol is a plausible concern but unverified for Mongolia.
- No confirmation that a STEPS 2023 round has been published.
- Iron-deficiency anemia, zinc, folate, fiber-intake figures: none retrieved.

## KQ4. Official Mongolian dietary guidance (FBDG, food guide, recommended intakes, meal distribution)

### Takeaway
Mongolia has official food-based dietary guidelines (first 1990, latest 2010) illustrated by a гэр (ger) divided into three consumption levels, legally approved recommended nutrient intakes ("Монгол хүний хоол тэжээлийн зөвлөмж хэмжээ"), and a national nutrition programme (Government Resolution 447, 2015). The NCPH recommends 2,100–2,500 kcal/day for adults, split breakfast 20–25%, lunch 35–40%, dinner ≤15–20%, with snacks 10–15%.

### Cited Findings
- Official dietary guidelines for Mongolians were first released in 1990; the latest version was published in 2010. The national food guide uses the гэр (Mongolian round tent) as its symbol, divided into three levels of recommended consumption. Guidelines recommend ≥3 servings vegetables and ≥2 servings fruit per day. — [FAO FBDG Mongolia (EN)](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/dietary-guidelines/regions/mongolia/en/); [FAO FBDG country page](https://www.fao.org/nutrition/education/food-dietary-guidelines/regions/countries/mongolia/)
- "Монгол хүний хоол тэжээлийн зөвлөмж хэмжээ" (recommended intakes) are approved as recommended energy, macronutrient, vitamin and mineral amounts needed for normal function, growth and energy expenditure of Mongolians. — [Legalinfo.mn, document 12552](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/12552); [annex](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail/12552/2/207686)
- National Centre for Public Health (Нийгмийн эрүүл мэндийн хүрээлэн/ҮНДЭСНИЙ ТӨВ) advice: an adult should obtain on average 2,100–2,500 kcal/day from food, distributed as breakfast 20–25%, mid-morning/afternoon snacks 10–15%, lunch 35–40%, dinner not more than 15–20%. — reported by [gogo.mn](https://gogo.mn/r/192708) and [ikon.mn (2024-08-02)](https://ikon.mn/n/37da) (attribution between these two pages is from the search summary; verify)
- The Government of Mongolia approved the national programme "Хүн амын хоол тэжээл" (Population Nutrition) by Resolution No. 447 of 2015; its goal is to raise nutrition literacy, provide safe healthy food and reduce diet-related disease. — [Legalinfo.mn lawId 4826](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=4826)
- A Mongolian State University of Education paper discusses the dietary guidelines/food-guide topic (content not in snippet). — [MSUE Lavai 19-LAV-013](https://lavai.msue.edu.mn/ppapers/19-LAV-013.pdf)

### Inferences
- The official meal-split (lunch as the largest meal, light dinner) is culturally congruent and can be used verbatim in the app instead of imported "6 small meals" or 16:8 templates; it also indirectly addresses the big-dinner habit without a "no food after 18:00" rule.
- Because the FBDG date from 2010, the app can reasonably present WHO targets (salt <5 g, ≥400 g fruit+veg, free sugars <10% energy) alongside the national guidance; the national "≥3 veg + ≥2 fruit" is roughly aligned with 400 g/day.

### Gaps
- The exact three levels of the гэр food guide (which foods in each tier, serving counts for cereals/meat/dairy) were not retrieved.
- Recommended protein g/kg, fat %, salt and vitamin D values in the legal "зөвлөмж хэмжээ" were not retrieved (page blocked).
- No school-meal (Үдийн цай) or workplace nutrition standards were found this session.
- Whether a revised FBDG (post-2020) has been issued could not be confirmed.

## KQ5. Seasonality, feasting/fasting and traditional food beliefs

### Takeaway
Only indirect evidence was retrieved: a strong seasonal swing in vitamin D (summer 22.5 → winter 7.7 ng/mL) documents the physiological effect of Mongolia's winter, and tea is confirmed as the dominant beverage. The цагаан идээ (summer "white" dairy season) / улаан идээ (winter "red" meat season) pattern, Цагаан сар feasting, Buddhist мацаг fasting and "hot/cold" food beliefs are well known culturally but were not confirmed by any retrieved source this session.

### Cited Findings
- Seasonal physiology: mean 25(OH)D in adults fell from 22.5 ng/mL (summer) to 7.7 ng/mL (winter) with 80.1% <10 ng/mL in winter. — [PMC5083980](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5083980/)
- Tea (цай) is the main beverage for Mongolian children and adolescents of all ages and sexes. — [CAJMS](https://mongoliajol.info/index.php/CAJMS/article/view/2650)
- Mongolian cuisine is described as predominantly dairy, meat and animal fats. — [Atlantis Press IBCM 2025](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/ibcm-25/126016412)
- General cuisine descriptions (бууз national dish, хуушуур deep-fried pastry, цуйван noodles with mutton and vegetables). — [Wikipedia: Mongolian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_cuisine)

### Inferences
- The app should schedule its "add vitamin D / get midday sun" nudges from October to April, and its dairy-protein suggestions (ааруул, аарц, тараг, айраг) most heavily from June to September when fresh цагаан идээ is abundant and cheap (seasonal availability itself is unverified here).
- Feast periods (Цагаан сар in Jan–Feb, Наадам in July) are the natural points for "maintenance, not deficit" programming and alcohol-moderation content.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source for: цагаан идээ summer / улаан идээ winter seasonal pattern; Цагаан сар бууз quantities and typical intake; Buddhist мацаг (fasting) practice among lay Mongolians; traditional "халуун/хүйтэн" (hot/cold) food classification; the habit of drinking hot water or hot tea rather than cold water; winter produce availability and prices vs summer. All of these should be confirmed with a follow-up search (Mongolian-language queries on ikon.mn / gogo.mn / news.mn and ethnographic literature).

## KQ6. Budget: food spending, meat and produce prices 2025–2026, cheap protein-adequate diet

### Takeaway
Meat prices rose sharply in 2025–2026: bone-in mutton ~16,500 ₮/kg and bone-in beef ~19,700 ₮/kg (Feb 2025), boneless beef ~24,300 ₮/kg (Aug 2025) and ~30,000 ₮/kg by early 2026 (+8,000 ₮ YoY), prompting city "reserve meat" sales at 13,000–15,000 ₮/kg. Vegetables are cheap per kg (potato ~3,150 ₮, carrot ~3,000 ₮, cabbage ~3,750 ₮ in Mar 2026) and eggs ~612 ₮ each. Household food-share figures were not retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- 17 Feb 2025 average Ulaanbaatar prices: хонины ястай мах (bone-in mutton) 16,511 ₮/kg; үхрийн ястай мах (bone-in beef) 19,680 ₮/kg. — [ikon.mn (dated 2025-02-07 item)](https://ikon.mn/n/3cax) / [ikon.mn](https://ikon.mn/n/3lyi) (search summary did not pin which page carried which number)

### Inferences
- Cost per gram of protein (estimates using the prices above and generic composition: beef ~20 g protein/100 g raw boneless; egg ~6.5 g protein each): boneless beef at 30,000 ₮/kg ≈ 150 ₮ per g protein; eggs at 612 ₮ ≈ 95 ₮ per g protein; reserve mutton at 13,000 ₮/kg bone-in (≈16 g protein/100 g edible after bone loss, rough) ≈ 100–130 ₮ per g protein. Eggs are therefore currently the cheapest widely available animal protein in UB, and bone-in reserve meat beats boneless retail cuts. **All estimates.**
- Vegetables at ~3,000–3,800 ₮/kg mean a WHO-level 400 g/day of the "4 vegetables" costs only ~1,300–1,500 ₮/day — budget is not the barrier to vegetable intake; habit and winter variety are.
- With meat inflation of ~50% in 2025, "eat more lean meat" advice is now budget-sensitive; the app should frame protein targets around eggs, dairy curds (ааруул/аарц), bone-in reserve meat, and dried/whole milk rather than premium cuts.

### Gaps
- Prices for chicken (тахианы мах), fish (загас), horse meat (адууны мах), goat (ямааны мах), milk (сүү), тараг, ааруул, flour (гурил), rice (будаа), fruit (алим etc.) in 2025–2026 were not retrieved.
- Household food expenditure as a share of total consumption (HSES 2023/2024), and typical monthly food spend per household, not retrieved.
- Rural vs UB price differences not found.

## KQ7. How Mongolian fitness influencers, dietitians and gyms currently advise clients; common local diet myths

### Takeaway
No sources were retrieved on this question (search budget exhausted before the Mongolian-language queries on "6 цагаас хойш хоол идэхгүй", detox teas, buckwheat diets ran). The only adjacent evidence is that even medical professionals in Mongolia often did not know the recommended salt limit, suggesting low baseline nutrition literacy among advice-givers.

### Cited Findings
- A cross-sectional study found many Mongolian medical professionals lacked knowledge of the recommended daily salt intake, and analysed factors related to that gap. — [PMC8067531](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8067531/)
- The 2015 national nutrition programme explicitly targets raising population nutrition-health literacy. — [Legalinfo.mn lawId 4826](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=4826)

### Inferences
- If clinicians' salt knowledge is weak, lay influencer advice is unlikely to be stronger; the app should assume users have absorbed generic imported myths and should counter them with the official NCPH meal-split (lunch-heavy, light dinner) rather than with a time cutoff.

### Gaps
- No retrieved evidence on prevalence of local myths ("орой 6 цагаас хойш хоол идэхгүй" / no eating after 6 pm, "сагаган дэглэм" buckwheat diet, detox цай, "усны дэглэм", "уураг уух" protein-shake fixation), nor on how UB gyms/dietitians (e.g., clinics' хоол тэжээлийн эмч) set protein or calorie targets. ## KQ8. Practical adaptation of fitness-nutrition advice (protein, vegetables, fiber, deficits, sodium, hydration, holidays)

### Takeaway
The evidence base supports four adaptations: (1) cut sodium via tea salt and processed meat rather than table salt; (2) build protein targets on eggs, dairy curds and bone-in meat because of 2025–26 meat inflation; (3) treat vegetables (not fruit) as the realistic fiber lever, using the cheap "4 vegetables"; (4) assume winter vitamin D deficiency for UB indoor exercisers. Recipe-level tweaks (leaner бууз, less oil in хуушуур/цуйван) are reasonable inferences but no published Mongolian "healthier recipe" analysis was found.

### Cited Findings
- Sodium sources to target: salty milk tea, sausages, processed meats, bread. — [J Hypertension abstract](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/jhypertension/fulltext/10.1097/01.hjh.0000420503.99692.39~1043-salt-intake-and-sources-of-salt-in-the-diet-in); ≥2 cups/day salted сүүтэй цай raises odds of exceeding 5 g salt. — [Sci Rep 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-37631-x)
- Low fruit intake (40% none weekly) vs more regular vegetable intake (48% daily). — [WHO STEPS 2019 microdata](https://extranet.who.int/ncdsmicrodata/index.php/catalog/836)
- Vitamin D: winter deficiency near-universal in a 2011–13 adult sample; review recommends fortification + supplementation. — [PMC5083980](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5083980/); [PMC6835745](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6835745/)
- Official meal split: breakfast 20–25%, lunch 35–40%, dinner ≤15–20%, snacks 10–15%, 2,100–2,500 kcal/day for adults. — [gogo.mn](https://gogo.mn/r/192708)
- Fermented dairy (тараг, айраг, ааруул, бяслаг, ээзгий) is rich in live lactic-acid bacteria; ааруул is concentrated in protein and calcium. — [Milk Science](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/milk/55/2/55_79/_article); [SLU thesis](https://stud.epsilon.slu.se/8197/)
- Current prices make eggs (~612 ₮) and vegetables (~3,000–3,800 ₮/kg) cheap relative to boneless beef (~30,000 ₮/kg). — [gogo.mn](https://gogo.mn/r/161690); [ikon.mn](https://ikon.mn/n/3mhg)
- Local soft drinks carry 10–13 g carbohydrate per 100 ml (≈ 50–64 g sugar per 500 ml bottle). — [NUM journal](https://journal.num.edu.mn/soc/article/view/9772)

### Inferences (practical rules the app could implement; all are my synthesis, label as "estimates" in-app)
- **Protein target.** Keep the standard 1.2–1.6 g/kg for recreational trainees (estimate; standard sports-nutrition range, not a Mongolian source) but express it in local units: e.g., for a 70 kg person ≈ 85–110 g/day = "2 eggs + 1 cup тараг at breakfast, a palm-size (~150 g) portion of lean meat at lunch, 40–50 g ааруул or аарц as an afternoon snack, and a lighter dinner". Dried curds as the between-meal protein replaces imported protein-bar advice.
- **Leaner бууз/хуушуур/цуйван.** Use үхрийн цул мах or ямааны мах instead of fatty хонины мах, trim өөх (tail fat), add grated байцаа/лууван/сонгино to the filling (raises volume and fiber, cuts kcal per piece), steam rather than fry (бууз over хуушуур), and in цуйван halve the oil and double the vegetables. These follow from the composition logic above; no analysed "light" recipe was found.
- **Vegetables in winter.** Target 400 g/day from the "4 vegetables" (төмс, лууван, байцаа, сонгино) plus frozen mixed vegetables and fermented cabbage (даршилсан байцаа / кимчи), counting potato as a starch not a vegetable. Pickles/sauerkraut add fiber but also sodium — fits a diet only if tea salt is reduced.
- **Sodium.** Most impactful single change: unsalted or lightly salted сүүтэй цай (users drink several cups), then limit хиам (sausage) and salty broth (шөл) toppings. Show daily sodium in grams of salt against the 5 g WHO line.
- **Calorie deficit.** Follow the NCPH meal split: make lunch the largest meal (цуйван/шөл with extra vegetables) and shrink dinner, instead of imposing a "no food after 18:00" rule; a 300–500 kcal deficit from a 2,100–2,500 kcal baseline is the natural default.
- **Hydration.** Count unsalted tea and plain hot water toward fluid; count salted tea as sodium; flag soft drinks (≈50–60 g sugar per 500 ml).
- **Vitamin D.** Oct–Apr: suggest fortified milk/supplement discussion with a clinician and midday outdoor walks; May–Sep: outdoor training for sun exposure.
- **Holidays.** Цагаан сар / Наадам: switch to maintenance targets, "3 small бууз then vegetables/fruit" pacing, alternate alcoholic drinks with tea or water. No sourced Mongolian guidance for this was found.

### Gaps
- No published Mongolian "healthier recipe" nutrient analysis for бууз/хуушуур/цуйван.
- No Mongolian data on fiber intake, whole-grain availability (e.g., bran bread, хар талх), or legume consumption.
- No Mongolian dietitian or MoH guidance on alcohol during Наадам/Цагаан сар retrieved.
- Protein g/kg recommendations in the Mongolian legal norms not retrieved.
