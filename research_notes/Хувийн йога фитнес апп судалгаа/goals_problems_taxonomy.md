# Taxonomy of user goals and health problems for a personalized home fitness / yoga / pilates app (Mongolia-focused)

Research note for the report writer. Scope: distinct goals/problems the app must address, and for each: who has it, what evidence says works (type, frequency, intensity, time-to-effect), what to avoid, nutrition emphasis, measurable outcomes, and how the program must differ from other goals.

Method note: 30 web searches were completed; the shared per-turn search budget was then exhausted and every attempted page fetch (WHO, PubMed/PMC, Cochrane, BJSM, ACOG, Healio) was blocked by the network proxy. Facts below are therefore drawn from search-result summaries of the primary papers/guidelines, each with its URL. Where a well-known figure could not be retrieved this session it is listed under Gaps rather than asserted, so the writer can verify or drop it.

Cross-cutting baseline (applies to every goal unless the section says otherwise): WHO 2020 recommends adults do 150–300 min/week moderate or 75–150 min/week vigorous aerobic activity plus muscle-strengthening on 2+ days/week; adults 65+ add multicomponent balance/strength activity on 3+ days/week; children/adolescents 5–17 average 60 min/day MVPA with muscle- and bone-strengthening 3 days/week — [WHO news release, 25 Nov 2020](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-11-2020-every-move-counts-towards-better-health-says-who); [University of Sydney summary](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2020/11/26/new-global-guidelines-on-physical-activity-and-sedentary-behavio.html).

## 1. Fat loss / weight management (incl. GLP-1 users, regain prevention, exercise-vs-diet contribution)

### Takeaway
Diet drives the deficit; exercise alone yields only ~0–2 kg long-term, but exercise is what preserves lean mass, cardiorespiratory fitness and — at 200–300 min/week — prevents regain. GLP-1 users are a distinct sub-profile: large, fast weight loss with 25–40 % of it potentially lean mass, so their program is strength-first with high protein, not cardio-first.

### Cited Findings
- Long-term (>6 months) exercise-only weight loss is roughly 0–2 kg; adding exercise to diet gives ~1 kg extra (Cochrane: WMD −1.0 kg, 95 % CI −1.3 to −0.7) and higher intensity adds more (WMD −1.5 kg) — [Shaw et al. Cochrane review via Unbound Medicine](https://www.unboundmedicine.com/medline/citation/15925949/full_citation); [PMC9017288](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9017288)
- Diet-plus-exercise vs diet-only at long-term follow-up: pooled extra loss 1.14 kg (95 % CI 0.21–2.07) — [Wu et al. 2009 meta-analysis, PubMed 19175510](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19175510/)
- ACSM position stand: ≥150 min/week moderate activity improves health in overweight/obese adults; 200–300 min/week is recommended for long-term weight loss and for maintenance of lost weight (≈40–60 min, 5 days/week). The ACSM itself notes the maintenance figure rests on National Weight Control Registry and questionnaire-based data with "major flaws" in design — [IDEA Fitness summary of ACSM stand](https://www.ideafit.com/acsm-on-weight-loss); [StatPearls NBK565813](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK565813/); [Clinical Nutrition Center evidence update](https://www.clinicalnutritioncenter.com/weightloss/medical-weight-loss-news/exercise-for-weight-loss-maintenance)
- HIIT vs moderate continuous training in overweight/obese adults 18–45 (13 studies, 786 participants): essentially identical fat loss (~2.6 kg each); HIIT needed ~40 % less time — [Wewege et al. 2017 via MedicalXpress](https://m.medicalxpress.com/news/2017-04-moderate-high-intensity-similar-results.html); [2023 update meta-analysis PMC10048683](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10048683/)
- Protein during energy restriction: intakes up to ~1.9 g/kg body mass/day (≈2.5 g/kg fat-free mass) are associated with less fat-free-mass loss; a 2018 meta-analysis found no further lean-mass advantage above 1.6 g/kg/day; ~1.6 g/kg/day maximizes FFM gain with resistance training — [Examine.com review](https://examine.com/articles/dieting-with-a-side-of-extra-protein); [Biolayne summary](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-34/maximizing-muscle-retention-with-protein/); [PubMed 26883880](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26883880/)
- GLP-1 receptor agonists: a 2022 meta-analysis found 25–40 % of weight lost was lean mass; a 2026 International Journal of Obesity meta-analysis found fat loss dominant and lean-mass loss "generally modest"; the lean:fat ratio is not categorically different from non-drug weight loss, the difference is the magnitude — [ACE Fitness, June 2025](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/june-2025/8892/glp-1s-and-lean-mass-what-the-research-shows/); [Substance over Noise analysis](https://substance-over-noise.beehiiv.com/p/how-much-muscle-are-glp-1-patients-actually-losing); [CED Clinic RCT summary](https://cedclinic.com/glp-1-receptor-agonist-randomized-trial-muscle-loss-risk/)
- Practitioner guidance for GLP-1 users: resistance training 2–4 sessions/week covering all major muscle groups plus protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day to preserve muscle, bone density and resting metabolic rate — [Carbon/GLP-1 guidance](https://www.joincarbon.com/blog/glp-1-weight-loss-and-muscle-loss-how-to-protect-lean-mass); [ACE Fitness](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/june-2025/8892/glp-1s-and-lean-mass-what-the-research-shows/)
- Visceral fat responds to aerobic exercise but not to resistance training alone (meta-analysis, >2,100 participants): significant pooled effect for aerobic vs control, non-significant for progressive resistance training — [Ismail et al. 2012 meta-analysis (ECU repository)](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2012/325); [PDF copy](https://accurateclinic.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/A-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis-of-the-effect-of-aerobic-vs.-resistance-exercise-training-on-visceral-fat-PubMed-2012.pdf)

### Inferences
- For "lose 8 kg" the program's lever is a sustained energy deficit (nutrition module + daily steps/NEAT), while the training block is 2–3 full-body strength sessions/week to protect lean mass and 150→250 min/week of low-impact aerobic work built up over 8–12 weeks. Expect ~0.5 kg/week from diet; exercise alone will not deliver 8 kg.
- Weight-regain prevention is a separate phase: once goal weight is reached the app should raise, not lower, the activity target (toward 200–300 min/week) and shift from deficit to maintenance calories.
- GLP-1 profile differs from ordinary fat loss in three ways: (a) strength is the primary modality, cardio secondary; (b) protein target is set per kg of goal/lean weight because appetite suppression makes hitting 1.2–1.6 g/kg hard — prompt protein-first meals; (c) monitor for excessive fatigue/nausea around sessions and keep sessions short (20–30 min).
- Measurable outcomes: body weight trend (7-day average), waist circumference (visceral proxy, important given Mongolia's 37 % normal-weight central obesity — see §10), protein grams/day, strength benchmarks (push-up/squat reps), weekly active minutes.
- Avoid: crash deficits (>1 kg/week), cardio-only plans, HIIT as the default for sedentary obese beginners (equivalent fat loss but higher joint/injury load — see §13).

### Gaps
- National Weight Control Registry figures (≈1 h/day activity in maintainers) not retrieved this session.
- The 2021 NEJM trial of liraglutide ± exercise (Lundgren et al.) showing combined therapy preserved lean mass and doubled fat loss could not be fetched (ACE page blocked); writer should verify before citing.
- No Mongolian-diet-specific nutrition trial located; nutrition advice here is generic (protein, deficit, fruit/veg).

## 2. Muscle gain / toning / strength for beginners at home with minimal equipment

### Takeaway
Untrained people get most of the available strength and near-maximal hypertrophy from just 2 sessions/week of multiple moderate-load sets; the minimum detectable dose is ~4 sets/muscle/week for growth and 1 set/week for strength, with 5–10 sets/muscle/week as the practical sweet spot for the first months.

### Cited Findings
- Minimum effective dose: ~4 sets per muscle per week for detectable hypertrophy; 1 set per week for small but detectable strength gain — [Sci-Sport review of minimum-dose evidence](https://sci-sport.com/en/resistance-training-minimum-dose-for-better-health-241/)
- Untrained individuals: moderate loads, multiple sets, 2 sessions/week captured most of the strength benefit, near-maximal hypertrophy and the full mobility improvement seen with more demanding protocols — [Peter Attia MD summary of the trial/meta-analytic evidence](https://peterattiamd.com/lowering-the-barrier-to-entry-for-resistance-training/)
- Weekly set volume correlates with growth with diminishing returns beyond ~12–20 sets/muscle/week; 5–10 sets/week/muscle is the best range for most; 2–3 sets vs 1 set per exercise gives ~40 % greater strength and hypertrophy gains — [Springer meta-analysis (Sports Medicine 2017)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-017-0762-7/fulltext.html); [MDPI Sports 2018](https://dx.doi.org/10.3390/sports6010007); [Biolayne volume review](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-36/too-much-muscle-math-finding-the-ideal-training-volume-per-session/)
- Guidelines recommend untrained people perform 1–3 sets per exercise vs 3–6 for advanced — [Springer meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-017-0762-7/fulltext.html)
- Protein ~1.6 g/kg/day maximizes fat-free-mass gain with resistance training — [Examine.com](https://examine.com/articles/dieting-with-a-side-of-extra-protein)

### Inferences
- Beginner "toning" program: 2 (progressing to 3) full-body sessions/week, 4–6 compound bodyweight/band movements, 2–3 sets of 8–15 reps taken near failure (last 2–3 reps hard), 6–8 sets/muscle/week in weeks 1–4 rising to ~10. Visible/strength changes typically reported at 8–12 weeks; strength gains earlier (neural).
- Progression at home without weights: tempo, range (deficit push-ups, Bulgarian split squats), unilateral work, resistance bands, backpack loading, rep-in-reserve targets.
- Nutrition: 1.6 g/kg protein at maintenance or small surplus (+200–300 kcal) — distinct from the deficit used in §1.
- Outcomes: reps-to-failure tests (push-up, squat, inverted row), band resistance level, limb circumference, photos every 4 weeks.
- Differs from fat loss: calories at or above maintenance, less cardio; differs from older-adult sarcopenia (§9) in intensity/failure proximity and lack of balance component.

### Gaps
- Exact author/title of the Springer (10.1007/s40279-017-0762-7) and MDPI Sports 6(1):7 papers not confirmed from the page; writer should check attribution (likely Schoenfeld/Krieger dose-response work).
- No home-based, equipment-free RCT specifically in Mongolian or Asian untrained adults found.

## 3. Posture, neck/shoulder tension and non-specific low back pain in desk workers

### Takeaway
For chronic non-specific LBP, yoga, Pilates and motor-control exercise all beat doing nothing (low–moderate certainty, small–medium effects) and none is clearly superior to the others; for neck/shoulder pain in office workers, high-intensity specific strength training totalling ~1 h/week — split into 3×20 or even 9×7 min — reduced pain over 20 weeks, and minimally supervised versions still worked.

### Cited Findings
- Cochrane yoga for chronic non-specific LBP: 12 RCTs, 1,080 participants aged 34–48 (India, UK, US); yoga reduced pain and improved function vs no exercise in the short term; only low-to-moderate quality evidence up to 6 months; programs were LBP-specific and led by experienced teachers — [Wieland et al. Cochrane 2017, PMC5726825](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5726825/); [Cochrane summary](https://es.cochrane.org/en/news/yoga-may-have-health-benefits-people-chronic-non-specific-lower-back-pain); [Evidently Cochrane](https://evidentlycochrane.net/?p=6866)
- Cochrane Pilates for LBP (Yamato et al.): 10 trials, 510 participants; vs minimal intervention Pilates reduced pain at short and intermediate term (medium effect) and disability (small→medium effect), low-to-moderate quality; unclear whether Pilates is superior to other exercise — [Macquarie University record](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/pilates-for-low-back-pain-complete-republication-of-a-cochrane-re/); [AAFP 2016 summary](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2016/0401/p556)
- Motor control exercise (deep trunk activation progressing to functional tasks) is the subject of a separate Cochrane review by the same group — [George Institute record](https://www.georgeinstitute.org.au/publications/motor-control-exercise-for-non-specific-low-back-pain-a-cochrane-review)
- Office workers (n = 447) randomized to 1×60, 3×20 or 9×7 min/week of supervised high-intensity neck/shoulder strength training for 20 weeks vs reference: all training groups reduced neck and right-shoulder pain vs reference — [Andersen et al., University of Copenhagen record](https://www.ucviden.dk/en/publications/influence-of-frequency-and-duration-of-strength-training-for-effe/); [PEDro record](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/32942)
- Follow-up cluster RCT (n = 351 office workers, 3×/week for 20 weeks) with minimal supervision still significantly reduced neck-pain intensity and headache vs reference — [Copenhagen record](https://www.ucviden.dk/en/publications/effect-of-training-supervision-on-effectiveness-of-strength-train/)
- Walking, yoga and strength training were among the most effective exercise types for depression (relevant because chronic pain and low mood co-occur) — [Noetel et al. BMJ 2024 via PsyPost](https://psypost.org/physical-activity-and-mental-health-exercises-therapeutic-potential-for-depression-highlighted-in-new-meta-analysis)

### Inferences
- Desk-worker program = three layers: (1) daily 7–10 min micro-sessions of band/isometric neck-shoulder strengthening (shrugs, reverse flys, lateral raises, wall slides) — the 9×7 min arm shows this is as effective as a weekly hour; (2) 2–3×/week 20–30 min trunk motor-control/Pilates/yoga-for-LBP sequence; (3) hourly movement breaks. Expect pain change at 8–12 weeks, robust by 20 weeks.
- "Posture" should be framed as strength + variability, not holding one correct position; evidence is for strengthening and graded exposure, not posture correction per se.
- Avoid: bed rest, fear-based cueing ("protect your spine"), passive-only stretching, loaded end-range flexion first thing in the morning for symptomatic users, unsupervised advanced yoga backbends in acute flare.
- Nutrition is secondary; if the user is overweight, pair with §1 since load reduction helps.
- Outcomes: 0–10 pain score weekly, Oswestry/Roland-Morris style function questions, neck pain days/month, headache days, sit-to-stand and plank time.
- Differs from flexibility goal (§4): emphasis on loading and control, not range; differs from fat loss: no deficit required.

### Gaps
- NICE NG59 specific recommendations (group exercise, no acupuncture/belts, consider CBT) not retrieved; search did not return NICE pages.
- McGill "Big 3" (curl-up, side plank, bird-dog) RCT evidence not found in this session's results.
- Andersen's 2-min/day elastic-tubing trial (2011) not retrievable.
- No trial of sit-stand desks/movement breaks on musculoskeletal pain retrieved.

## 4. Flexibility & mobility (hips, hamstrings, shoulders, thoracic) and splits/back-bend goals

### Takeaway
Total weekly stretching time, not minutes per session, predicts range-of-motion gain: ≥5 min/week per muscle group, spread over ≥5 days/week, with static stretching, improves ROM; acute stretching gives temporary ROM, and gains regress after detraining.

### Cited Findings
- Thomas et al. 2018 (Int J Sports Med 39:243–254): weekly time spent stretching is fundamental to ROM gain when ≥5 min/week per muscle; time within a single session did not significantly affect gains; frequency positively associated with ROM; stretching ≥5 days/week for ≥5 min/week using static stretching recommended — [PubMed 29506306](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29506306/); [IAT record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4047878?lng=en)
- Acute (single-bout) effects of various stretching techniques on ROM reviewed in a 2023 meta-analysis — [PubMed 37962709](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37962709/)
- Warneke et al. 2024–2025 systematic reviews cover chronic stretching effects and detraining (loss of ROM after stopping) — [PMC12638565](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12638565); [University of Palermo detraining PDF](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/0b5511d2-af8c-47e0-b9d4-6999f7fd7d73/Stretching%20detraining.pdf)
- Resistance training through full range in untrained people delivered the full mobility improvement seen with more demanding protocols — [Peter Attia MD summary](https://peterattiamd.com/lowering-the-barrier-to-entry-for-resistance-training/)

### Inferences
- Program: daily 10–15 min, 2–3 target areas, 30–60 s holds × 2–4 per muscle, 5–7 days/week; measurable ROM change in 3–4 weeks, meaningful change (e.g., toward splits) in 8–12+ weeks; include loaded end-range strength (e.g., Jefferson curls, Cossack squats, pancake good-mornings) because full-ROM strength training improves mobility too.
- Splits/back-bend as goals: treat as long-horizon (months), with weekly photo/measurement checkpoints; back-bends need thoracic extension + hip-flexor length + shoulder flexion, not lumbar hyperextension.
- Avoid: ballistic bouncing for beginners, stretching into sharp nerve pain, deep passive back-bends for users with osteoporosis (§9) or acute LBP (§3), aggressive hamstring stretching in people with sciatica symptoms.
- Nutrition: none specific; hydration and adequate protein for tissue recovery.
- Outcomes: sit-and-reach cm, hip-flexor (Thomas test angle, phone-camera), shoulder flexion against wall, thoracic rotation degrees, distance-to-floor in splits.
- Differs from LBP program (§3): here range is the target; there control/strength is. Differs from all others by requiring daily frequency.

### Gaps
- No study found on timelines for achieving full splits or specific back-bend progressions; these are coaching heuristics, not evidence.
- No specific thoracic-mobility meta-analysis retrieved.

## 5. Stress, anxiety, sleep quality (yoga, breathwork, yoga nidra; exercise timing)

### Takeaway
Breathwork gives small–medium reductions in stress/anxiety/depression (g ≈ −0.32 to −0.40), yoga nidra shows large but probably inflated effects on anxiety, and evening exercise does not harm sleep in healthy adults — except vigorous exercise ending within ~1 h of bedtime.

### Cited Findings
- Breathwork meta-analysis of RCTs (Fincham et al. 2023, Scientific Reports 13:432): stress g = −0.35, anxiety g = −0.32, depression g = −0.40 vs controls — [PMC9828383](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9828383/); [Sussex figshare PDF](https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/journal_contribution/Effect_of_breathwork_on_stress_and_mental_health_a_meta-analysis_of_randomised-controlled_trials/23494097/1); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/breathwork-shows-promise-in-reducing-stress-anxiety-and-depression-according-to-new-meta-analysis)
- Yoga nidra meta-analysis (73 studies, 5,201 participants): anxiety effect −1.35 (active comparator) and −1.43 (no comparator); authors caution the moderate-to-large effects likely are inflated given low methodological quality and variable delivery — [PMC12917937](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12917937/); [PubMed 41327816](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41327816/)
- A systematic review of RCTs of yoga nidra for sleep disorders exists — [SAGE journal](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/27683605251390728)
- Stutz et al. 2018 (Sports Medicine) meta-analysis of 23 studies: evening exercise does not negatively affect sleep in healthy adults; non-vigorous evening exercise increased deep sleep; sleep-onset latency, total sleep time and efficiency may be impaired after vigorous exercise ending ≤1 h before bedtime — [BetterSleep summary](https://bettersleep.com/blog/morning-vs-evening-exercise-for-sleep-what-30-studies-say); [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/gb/blog/the-athletes-way/201812/easy-to-moderate-exercise-before-bedtime-promotes-deep-sleep); [FitChef study card](https://fitchef.com/studies/evening-exercise-does-not-ruin-sleep/)
- Exercise for depression network meta-analysis (BMJ 2024, 218 studies, >14,000 participants): walking/jogging, yoga and strength training more effective than other forms, more so when intense; yoga and strength training well tolerated — [Jyväskylä repository](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/93572); [PsyPost](https://psypost.org/physical-activity-and-mental-health-exercises-therapeutic-potential-for-depression-highlighted-in-new-meta-analysis)
- In high-school students, physical activity improved sleep quality which in turn lowered anxiety and stress — [Frontiers in Public Health 2025](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1651883/epub)

### Inferences
- Stress/sleep program: 20–30 min moderate yoga or strength 3–5×/week (any time of day), plus daily 5–10 min slow breathing (≈6 breaths/min) and 15–20 min yoga nidra at bedtime 3+ nights/week. Vigorous sessions end ≥1–2 h before bed; gentle yoga/stretch allowed right before bed. Expect subjective stress change within 2–4 weeks, sleep metrics at 4–8 weeks.
- Avoid: HIIT late at night for poor sleepers, caffeine-fuelled evening workouts, breath-holding practices (kumbhaka) for users with anxiety/panic, hypertension or pregnancy unless screened.
- Nutrition: regular meals, cut caffeine after midday, limit alcohol (relevant: Mongolia STEPS shows high alcohol use — §10), magnesium/omega-3 claims not evidenced here.
- Outcomes: PSQI-style sleep quality, sleep latency and wake time (self-report or phone), GAD-7 or PSS-10 every 2–4 weeks, resting HR / HRV if wearable.
- Differs from fat loss/strength: intensity is not the lever; consistency, breathing and timing are. Differs from LBP by including nidra/relaxation rather than loading.

### Gaps
- Exercise-for-insomnia meta-analyses (Kredlow 2015; Banno 2018) effect sizes not retrieved.
- No Mongolian-language or Mongolia-based trial of yoga/breathwork for stress found.

## 6. Energy / general fitness / "feel better", cardiovascular health, VO2max and longevity

### Takeaway
Cardiorespiratory fitness is the strongest single modifiable predictor of mortality (high vs low CRF: HR 0.47), each 1-MET gain cuts all-cause mortality 11–17 %, and mortality benefit from walking starts at ~4,000 steps/day with each extra 1,000 steps worth ~15 % — so a "feel better" program is a progressive aerobic base plus 2 strength days, measured by a field VO2 estimate and steps.

### Cited Findings
- Overview of meta-analyses (BJSM May 2024; 199 cohorts, 20.9 million observations): high vs low CRF all-cause mortality HR 0.47 (95 % CI 0.39–0.56); each 1-MET (≈3.5 ml/kg/min) increase → 11–17 % lower all-cause mortality, ~18 % lower heart-failure risk; low CRF linked to heart failure, cancer, dementia, diabetes, depression — [Lang et al. 2024, PubMed 38599681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38599681/); [PMC11103301](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11103301); [LabRoots summary](https://blog.labroots.com/trending/cardiology/27075/fitness-cuts-risk-death-disease-2)
- Steps meta-analysis (17 cohorts, 226,889 participants, median 7.1 y follow-up): all-cause mortality falls from ~3,967 steps/day, CV mortality from ~2,337; each +1,000 steps/day −15 % all-cause death, each +500 −7 % CV death; steepest benefit 6,000–10,000/day in >60 y, 7,000–13,000 in <60 y — [Banach et al. 2023 via MDLinx](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/just-4-000-steps-a-day-can-lower-your-risk-of-death-says-new-study/4WiaUHi6WS1jRSGnQoXQFb); [Medical Economics](https://medicaleconomics.com/view/new-study-finds-benefits-of-walking-start-at-just-2-400-steps-a-day)
- HIIT improves cardiorespiratory fitness with ~40 % less time than MICT at equal fat loss — [MedicalXpress on Wewege 2017](https://m.medicalxpress.com/news/2017-04-moderate-high-intensity-similar-results.html)
- Mongolia: low physical activity rose from 8.2 % (2005) to 28.4 % (2019) of adults 15–69 — [Mongolia STEPS trends, Iranian J Public Health](https://ijph.tums.ac.ir/index.php/ijph/article/download/27939/7987)

### Inferences
- "Feel better" program: 3 aerobic sessions/week (indoor: stair climbing, step-ups, dance/shadow-boxing, jump-rope-free low-impact cardio for apartment neighbours) building from 15→40 min, one of them intervals after week 4; 2 strength days; daily step target raised by 1,000/week toward 7,000–10,000. Fitness (estimated VO2max via 3-min step test or 1-mile walk test) measurably improves in 6–8 weeks; perceived energy often within 2–3 weeks.
- Winter constraint: outdoor steps collapse in Ulaanbaatar winters, so the app needs an indoor "step equivalent" (minutes of marching/stair-climbing) and should re-target to 150–300 min rather than steps from November to March.
- Avoid: jumping-only cardio in ger/apartment settings (noise/joint), sudden high-intensity for inactive >40 y with hypertension (§10 screening).
- Nutrition: no deficit needed; emphasize fruit/veg (79.5 % of Mongolians eat inadequate amounts — §10), hydration, less salt.
- Outcomes: resting HR, step-test recovery HR, 6-min walk distance or 1-mile walk time, steps/day, weekly MVPA minutes.
- Differs from fat loss: no calorie target; differs from strength: aerobic volume is primary.

### Gaps
- No direct source retrieved for the common practice of using the Rockport 1-mile walk test or 3-min step test for VO2 estimation; these are standard field tests but unsourced here.
- Ulaanbaatar PM2.5 thresholds for outdoor exercise not retrieved (search budget exhausted).

## 7. Women's health: cycle-aware training, PCOS, pregnancy, postpartum (diastasis recti, pelvic floor), perimenopause/menopause

### Takeaway
Cycle-syncing of strength training has no supporting evidence (program should be symptom-responsive, not phase-based); PCOS needs 150–300 min/week (≥250 for weight loss) plus 2 strength days; pregnancy allows ≥150 min/week moderate activity with specific avoidances; postpartum pelvic-floor training cuts incontinence odds 41 % and running returns at ~12 weeks; for postmenopausal low bone mass, supervised high-intensity resistance + impact (LIFTMOR) improved spine and hip BMD safely.

### Cited Findings
Menstrual cycle
- Colenso-Semple et al. 2023 (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living) systematic review: current evidence shows no influence of menstrual-cycle phase on acute strength performance or on adaptations to resistance training; studies suggesting cycle-syncing were small, poorly controlled and rarely verified phase hormonally — [Frontiers full text](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1054542/text); [PMC10076834](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10076834); [Stronger by Science analysis](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=61661); [Triathlete summary](https://www.triathlete.com/training/the-case-against-training-based-on-your-menstrual-cycle?scope=anon)

PCOS
- 2023 International PCOS Guideline: adults 150–300 min/week moderate or 75–150 vigorous plus muscle-strengthening on 2 non-consecutive days for health/weight-gain prevention; ≥250 min moderate or 150 vigorous/week plus 2 strength days for modest weight loss; adolescents ≥60 min/day MVPA with muscle/bone strengthening 3×/week; no single exercise type or intensity shown superior for metabolic, hormonal, reproductive or psychological outcomes — [PMC10958708](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10958708); [Dutch guideline adaptation](https://richtlijnendatabase.nl/richtlijn/adaptatietraject_internationale_richtlijn_pcos/leefstijl/beweeginterventies_.html)

Pregnancy
- ACOG Committee Opinion 804 (April 2020): in the absence of complications, exercise in pregnancy is safe and desirable; aim ≥150 min/week moderate aerobic activity spread through the week; women previously doing vigorous activity may continue; walking, stationary cycling, water aerobics suitable; avoid scuba diving and activities with abdominal-trauma risk; observational benefits include less gestational diabetes, fewer caesarean/operative deliveries, faster postpartum recovery and prevention of postpartum depressive disorders — [OPQIC summary of ACOG CO 804](https://opqic.org/acog-committee-opinion-no-804-physical-activity-and-exercise-during-pregnancy-and-the-postpartum-period/); [AMSSM case study](https://learn.amssm.org/AssetListing/Best-Practice-Case-Studies-1088/Exercise-in-Pregnancy-PS-4297)

Postpartum
- Beamish et al., BJSM 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of exercise in the first postpartum year: pelvic floor muscle training reduced urinary-incontinence odds by 41 % (8 RCTs, moderate certainty, moderate heterogeneity) but did not significantly improve symptom severity; insufficient evidence on diastasis recti abdominis, anal incontinence, prolapse or sexual function — [PubMed 39694630](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39694630/); [PMC12013572](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12013572/); [Physiotutors summary](https://www.physiotutors.com/?p=24410)
- Goom, Donnelly & Brockwell 2019 "Returning to Running Postnatal" guideline: wait until ~12 weeks postpartum before starting a graded return-to-running (e.g., Couch to 5K); evidence base limited, combines research with clinical opinion; used by 52 % of surveyed pelvic-health physiotherapists; women on average returned to running at 12 weeks — [Moving Medicine record](https://movingmedicine.ac.uk/evidence/goom-t-donnelly-g-brockwell-e-returning-to-running-postnatal-guidelines-for-medical-health-and-fitness-professionals-managing-this-population-march-2019/); [Athletics NI PDF](https://athleticsni.org/download/files/Returning_to_running_postnatal_guideline_for_medical_health_and_fitness_professionals_managing_this_population.05.pdf); [ICS 2024 abstract](https://www.ics.org/2024/abstract/712)

Perimenopause/menopause and bone
- LIFTMOR RCT (Watson et al., J Bone Miner Res 2018;33:211): brief, supervised high-intensity resistance and impact training in postmenopausal women with low–very low bone mass produced significantly greater lumbar-spine and femoral-neck BMD than a low-intensity control program, with no fractures or serious injuries — [JBMR article](https://academic.oup.com/jbmr/article/33/2/211/7605709); [Griffith repository](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/70307370-05b3-5d8b-8454-78b328f0f15b); [Herman & Wallace summary](https://www.hermanwallace.com/blog/liftmor-lifting-intervention-for-training-muscle-and-osteoporosis-rehab); [MelioGuide summary](https://melioguide.com/osteoporosis-exercises/liftmor-osteoporosis-osteopenia/)
- Too Fit to Fracture (Giangregorio et al. 2014, Osteoporosis Canada): strongly recommends multicomponent exercise with resistance and balance training for osteoporosis/vertebral fracture; for high-risk individuals use body weight or select exercises that avoid forward flexion/torsion of the spine; teach spine-sparing techniques — [PubMed 24281053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24281053/); [Osteoporosis Canada](https://osteoporosis.ca/exercise-recommendations); [PMC5101068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5101068)
- Exercise and quality of life in women with menopausal symptoms: systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs — [PMC7579592](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7579592/); mind-body exercise improved BMD, sleep, anxiety, depressive mood and fatigue in peri/postmenopausal women per a recent meta-analysis — [PMC11465887](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11465887)
- Hot flashes: evidence mixed; one trial (~250 women, Menopause journal) found exercise slightly reduced hot flashes but the sedentary control improved equally — [Cathe (secondary, low-quality source)](https://cathe.com/exercise-and-menopause-ways-exercise-improves-menopause/)

### Inferences
- Cycle: do not auto-change loads by phase. Offer optional symptom logging; if a user reports heavy dysmenorrhea or low energy, swap that day's session to mobility/breathing — personalization by symptom, not calendar.
- PCOS profile: volume target is higher than a generic fat-loss plan (≥250 min/week), strength on 2 days, with nutrition emphasis on reduced refined carbohydrate and a modest deficit if overweight; weight loss of 5–10 % is the usual clinical target (unsourced here — see gaps). Track cycle regularity, waist, weight, fasting glucose if available.
- Pregnancy: trimester-based program with RPE 12–14 ("talk test"), ≥150 min/week spread over most days, strength with bodyweight/bands, pelvic floor daily; exclude supine work after T1 if symptomatic, contact/fall-risk sports, hot yoga, breath-holding, deep twists/crunches late pregnancy. Red-flag list (bleeding, regular painful contractions, fluid leak, dizziness, chest pain, calf pain/swelling) must stop the session and route to care. (Specific contraindication list is in ACOG 804 but could not be fetched — see gaps.)
- Postpartum: weeks 0–6 pelvic floor + breathing + walking; weeks 6–12 progressive core (transverse/oblique activation, side planks, dead-bugs) and low-impact strength; impact/running from week 12 only if no leaking, heaviness or doming; DRA is monitored by finger-width/doming but evidence that exercise closes it is insufficient, so the app should promise function, not gap closure.
- Menopause/bone: a home app cannot replicate LIFTMOR's supervised 80–85 % 1RM barbell loading; it can prescribe the principles — heavy-ish progressive resistance for hip/spine (goblet/backpack squats, deadlift pattern, overhead press), impact (heel drops, small hops/jumps if cleared), balance — 2–3×/week; avoid loaded spinal flexion/twisting for known osteoporosis; protein ≥1.2 g/kg, calcium/vitamin D (Mongolia's winter sunlight is limited — relevant but unsourced). Bone changes need DXA at 12 months; proxies: grip strength, chair-rise, single-leg stance.
- Differs from generic strength (§2): impact and spine-sparing rules; differs from postpartum: no pelvic-floor gating.

### Gaps
- Full ACOG 804 contraindication and warning-sign lists not fetched (page blocked).
- Cochrane review of exercise for vasomotor symptoms (Daley et al.) not retrieved; the hot-flash evidence above comes from a non-academic source.
- No meta-analysis on exercise and inter-recti distance (DRA) retrieved; Beamish 2025 only states evidence is lacking.
- LIFTMOR protocol specifics (2×30 min/week, 8 months, 5 sets × 5 reps at >80–85 % 1RM; ~+2.9 % lumbar spine BMD) not verifiable this session.
- PCOS nutrition specifics (5–10 % weight loss target) not retrieved.

## 8. Men-specific: belly / visceral fat, low-testosterone concerns, "beer belly"

### Takeaway
Visceral fat responds to aerobic exercise (not resistance training alone) and to overall fat loss; exercise acutely bumps testosterone for <30 min but training does not raise resting testosterone in sedentary men — so the honest program is aerobic volume + diet (incl. alcohol reduction) + strength for body composition, and the app should not market "testosterone boosting".

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis (>2,100 individuals): aerobic exercise significantly reduced visceral fat vs control; progressive resistance training did not; direct comparison favoured aerobic but was non-significant — [Ismail et al. 2012 (ECU)](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2012/325); [FoundMyFitness episode](https://www.foundmyfitness.com/episodes/which-types-of-exercise-reduce-visceral-fat)
- Acute exercise raises total testosterone (serum and saliva) after moderate and high intensity, not mild; increase visible immediately and within 30 min, gone after 30 min — ["Endogenous transient doping" meta-analysis, PubMed 32297287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32297287/)
- Exercise training has a negligible effect on resting total testosterone in previously sedentary men; in older men endurance training showed a positive effect, resistance training none — [PubMed 30692929](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30692929/); [Frontiers in Physiology 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2018.01878/pdf)
- Mongolia 2019: normal-weight central obesity rose from 8.7 % (2005) to 37.0 % (2019) — [Population Medicine](https://www.populationmedicine.eu/Prevalence-and-associated-factors-of-normal-weight-central-obesity-among-community,233838,0,2.html); hyperuricemia prevalence 18 % in men vs 5 % in women — [ACR global hyperuricemia review](https://acrabstracts.org/abstract/global-prevalence-of-hyperuricemia-a-systematic-review-of-population-based-epidemiological-studies/)

### Inferences
- Program: 150→250 min/week aerobic (brisk walking/stairs/cycling) + 2–3 strength days; waist circumference is the primary KPI (not scale weight), target −1 cm/2 weeks; alcohol-unit logging because alcohol is both calories and a gout/hypertension driver in Mongolian men (§10).
- Avoid: ab-isolation "belly" workouts as the core plan (spot reduction unsupported; resistance alone did not cut visceral fat), testosterone-supplement upsells, extreme fasting that increases binge risk.
- Nutrition: modest deficit, protein 1.6 g/kg, cut sugary drinks and alcohol, more fibre/vegetables.
- Outcomes: waist (cm), waist-to-height ratio (<0.5), weight, BP, steps, alcohol units/week.
- Differs from women's fat loss mainly in KPI (waist/visceral), the alcohol module, and uric-acid/BP screening; differs from §2 in aerobic emphasis.

### Gaps
- Meta-analysis showing weight loss raises testosterone in obese men (Corona et al. 2013) not retrieved.
- Alcohol-and-abdominal-obesity dose data not retrieved.

## 9. Older adults 55+: sarcopenia, balance/falls, osteoporosis, knee osteoarthritis

### Takeaway
Resistance training is the proven treatment for sarcopenia (dozens of RCTs), the Otago home strength-and-balance program cuts falls by about a third and reduced 12-month mortality, exercise improves knee-OA pain and function (Cochrane), and WHO asks for 3+ multicomponent balance/strength days per week — so this program is strength + balance + protein, with spine-sparing rules for osteoporosis.

### Cited Findings
- Otago Exercise Programme (progressive home-based strength, balance, walking): reduces falls by over a third; meta-analysis of 7 trials (1,503 participants) found significantly reduced fall rate and risk of death over 12 months — [Region Västerbotten Otago manual](https://regionvasterbotten.se/VLL\Filer\Otago-Exercise-Programme2.pdf); [Flinders University record](https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/does-the-otago-exercise-programme-reduce-mortality-and-falls-in-o/); [Texas A&M evidence-based program listing](https://ebc.tamhsc.edu/program/otago-exercise-program)
- Cochrane exercise for knee OA: improvement in pain and physical function vs attention control/placebo, and in pain, function and quality of life vs no treatment/usual care; benefit of uncertain clinical importance — [Cochrane CD004376](https://www.cochrane.org/CD004376); [AAFP summary](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2015/1101/p774); [BJSM 2015 systematic review](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/49/24/1554)
- Sarcopenia: 14 RCTs (561 healthy older adults aged 65.8–82.8 with sarcopenia) confirm resistance training effective — [PMC8588688](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8588688/); network meta-analysis of 50 RCTs (4,085 participants): resistance training with or without nutrition improves strength and physical performance — [PMC10687931](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10687931/); dose-optimization review — [PMC12908291](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12908291/); handgrip dose network meta-analysis — [PMC12263917](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12263917/)
- Chair-based exercise (meta-analysis, 1,388 participants) improves handgrip, 30-s arm curl and 30-s chair stand; recommended as simple, easily implemented — [PMC7920319](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7920319/); [MDPI IJERPH 18(4):1902](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/18/4/1902)
- Too Fit to Fracture: multicomponent resistance + balance strongly recommended for osteoporosis; avoid forward flexion/torsion for high-risk; spine-sparing technique — [PubMed 24281053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24281053/); [Osteoporosis Canada](https://osteoporosis.ca/exercise-recommendations)
- LIFTMOR: supervised high-intensity resistance/impact improved BMD in postmenopausal women with low bone mass without injury — [JBMR 2018](https://academic.oup.com/jbmr/article/33/2/211/7605709)
- Steps: in >60 y, steepest mortality benefit between 6,000 and 10,000 steps/day — [Banach 2023 via MDLinx](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/just-4-000-steps-a-day-can-lower-your-risk-of-death-says-new-study/4WiaUHi6WS1jRSGnQoXQFb)
- WHO: adults 65+ should do multicomponent balance + strength ≥3 days/week — [WHO 2020](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-11-2020-every-move-counts-towards-better-health-says-who)

### Inferences
- Core program (55+): 3×/week 30–40 min Otago-style blocks — sit-to-stand, step-ups, heel/toe raises, hip abduction with band, tandem/single-leg stance, backward walking — plus daily walking; progress load every 2 weeks; chair-based entry tier for frail or obese users; strength gains measurable at 6–8 weeks, fall-risk markers at 12 weeks, BMD only at 12 months.
- Knee OA sub-profile: quadriceps/hip strengthening + low-impact aerobic (cycling, water if available), 3×/week, 8–12 weeks; pain flare ≤2 points on 0–10 that settles by next day is acceptable; avoid deep loaded flexion early, jumping, running on hard surfaces.
- Osteoporosis sub-profile: add impact (heel drops, stomps) only if no vertebral fracture; ban loaded forward bends, sit-ups, deep twists, toe-touch stretches.
- Nutrition: protein 1.2–1.5 g/kg spread across meals (sarcopenia reviews pair RT with nutrition), calcium/vitamin D, adequate calories (unintentional weight loss is a red flag).
- Outcomes: 30-s chair stand, timed up-and-go, single-leg stance seconds, grip (if dynamometer), 4-m gait speed, falls log, KOOS-style knee pain/function.
- Differs from all younger profiles: balance is mandatory, intensity progression is slower, exercises are seated/supported first, and spinal-flexion avoidance may apply.

### Gaps
- Exact sarcopenia RT dose (sessions/week, %1RM, weeks) from PMC12908291 not fetched; writer should pull numbers from the paper.
- Cochrane knee-OA effect sizes on 0–100 scales and GLA:D program outcomes not retrieved.
- Vitamin D status in Mongolian older adults not researched.

## 10. Metabolic conditions common in Mongolia: hypertension, prediabetes/T2D, dyslipidaemia, fatty liver, gout

### Takeaway
Mongolia's 2019 STEPS data show 23 % hypertension, 28 % raised cholesterol, 64 % overweight/obesity, 37 % normal-weight central obesity and 28 % physical inactivity; isometric training (e.g., wall squats) is the single most effective exercise mode for resting BP (−8.2/−4.0 mmHg), structured exercise lowers HbA1c by ~0.67 %, ≥150 min/week brisk walking cuts liver fat even without weight loss, and gout management is low-impact activity plus weight/alcohol control with hard stops during flares.

### Cited Findings
Mongolia burden
- Hypertension prevalence fell from 28.4 % (2005) to 23.2 % (2019); awareness unchanged, treatment among aware decreased, control improved — [PMC9388008](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9388008/)
- Overweight/obesity rose 49.6 % → 64.0 % (2005→2019); normal-weight central obesity 8.7 % → 37.0 %; diabetes prevalence unchanged 2005–2019; inadequate fruit/veg intake 85.1 % → 79.5 %; smoking 26.6 % → 24.1 %; low physical activity 8.2 % → 28.4 %; raised total cholesterol 23.9 % → 27.6 % — [STEPS trend analysis, Iranian J Public Health](https://ijph.tums.ac.ir/index.php/ijph/article/download/27939/7987); [Population Medicine NWCO](https://www.populationmedicine.eu/Prevalence-and-associated-factors-of-normal-weight-central-obesity-among-community,233838,0,2.html)
- High prevalence and low awareness/treatment/control of dyslipidaemia among Mongolians 15–69 in 2019 — [PMC9213429](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9213429/)
- STEPS 2019 fielded June–Sept 2019, 6,654 adults 15–69, multistage stratified sample — [WHO NCD Microdata catalog](https://extranet.who.int/ncdsmicrodata/index.php/catalog/836)
- Hyperuricemia in Mongolia: 18 % of men, 5 % of women — [ACR systematic review abstract](https://acrabstracts.org/abstract/global-prevalence-of-hyperuricemia-a-systematic-review-of-population-based-epidemiological-studies/); in Inner Mongolia young male recruits 38.9 % hyperuricemia with BMI ≥25, spirits/beer and alcohol ≥500 ml/week as independent risk factors — [Hohhot soldier survey](https://wjyx.magtechjournal.com/EN/Y2016/V27/I1/5)

Hypertension
- Edwards et al. 2023 BJSM network meta-analysis: isometric training −8.24 systolic / −4.0 diastolic mmHg vs aerobic −4.49/−2.53, combined −6.04/−2.54, dynamic resistance −4.55/−3.04, HIIT −4.08/−2.50; wall squat best sub-mode for systolic (SUCRA 90.4 %), running for diastolic (91.3 %) — [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/primary-care/20230817/running-wall-sits-most-effective-exercises-for-high-blood-pressure); [Lifespan.io](https://www.lifespan.io/news/analysis-isometric-exercises-are-best-for-blood-pressure/); [Training & Conditioning](https://training-conditioning.com/news/isometric-exercises-blood-pressure-study/)
- Safety: resting BP >180/110 mmHg → do not exercise until managed — [Livestrong (secondary)](https://www.livestrong.com/article/332641-exercise-contraindications-in-people-with-high-blood-pressure/)

Prediabetes / T2D
- Structured exercise (aerobic, resistance or combined) lowers HbA1c by ~0.67 % (126 RCTs, 7,155 participants, Hedges g −0.67) and improves lipids, BP, anthropometrics, insulin/HOMA-IR, inflammation — [MDedge summary](https://mdedge.com/content/structured-exercise-training-lowers-hba1c-067-1); [Bangladesh J Med Sci meta-analysis](https://www.banglajol.info/index.php/BJMS/article/view/85382)
- No dose–response between exercise amount and HbA1c improvement in interventions >12 weeks — [PMC9519659](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9519659/)
- Safety: do not start exercise with glucose >250 mg/dL (≈13.9 mmol/L) with moderate–high ketones; caution >300 mg/dL without ketones; avoid when unwell or glucose >16.7 mmol/L with urinary ketones — [ACSM-CEP study guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/acsm-cep/exercise-prescription/exrx-metabolic); [NST Heal](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/lifestyle/heal/2018/03/347280/heal-exercise-diabetics-and-exercise)

Fatty liver (NAFLD/MASLD)
- Meta-analysis: ≥30 % relative reduction in MRI liver fat achieved by 34 % of exercisers vs 13 % of controls; ≥750 MET-min/week (≈150 min brisk walking) gave greater reductions than lower doses; benefit independent of weight loss — [Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/gastroenterology/20230510/people-with-nafld-who-exercise-are-35-times-more-likely-to-achieve-liver-fat-reduction); [ConscienHealth](https://conscienhealth.org/2023/02/17/the-essential-value-of-exercise-in-nafld/); [MDedge](https://mdedge.com/content/exercise-training-reduces-liver-fat-patients-nafld-even-without-weight-loss)

Gout
- Joint-friendly activity (walking, cycling, swimming), ≥150 min/week moderate; avoid high-intensity exercise during and immediately after a flare (can raise urate); resistance training/lean mass may help urate handling — [Hinge Health](https://www.hingehealth.com/resources/articles/gout-physical-exercise/); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/fitness/gout-physical-exercise); [Henry Ford Health](https://prod-cd.henryford.com/blog/2024/03/benefits-of-exercise-for-gout)

### Inferences
- Hypertension profile: add an isometric block — wall squat (or handgrip) 4 × 2 min at a hard-but-holdable effort, 2 min rest, 3×/week, 8–12 weeks (protocol as commonly used in the Edwards trials; dose unverified here) — on top of 150 min aerobic; breathe continuously (no Valsalva), avoid heavy maximal lifts and inverted yoga poses while uncontrolled; home BP log morning/evening; stop if >180/110 or symptoms. Nutrition: salt reduction (Mongolian diet is high in salted tea/meat — unsourced), DASH-style pattern, alcohol reduction. Expect BP changes in 4–8 weeks.
- Prediabetes/T2D profile: because there is no dose–response for HbA1c, the lever is regularity — ≥3 non-consecutive days, never >2 days without activity, 10–15 min post-meal walks, 2 strength days; glucose check before sessions for insulin/sulfonylurea users; foot inspection; carry carbohydrate. Nutrition: carbohydrate quality/quantity, fibre, weight loss 5–7 % if overweight. HbA1c re-check at 12 weeks.
- Dyslipidaemia: exercise effects on LDL are modest; program is the general fitness/fat-loss plan with nutrition emphasis (saturated fat from fatty mutton/dairy — unsourced), and lipid re-test at 3 months.
- Fatty liver: 150+ min/week brisk aerobic from the start, even if weight does not change; plus limit sugar/alcohol; outcome: liver enzymes/ultrasound at 3–6 months, waist.
- Gout: low-impact cardio + strength; flare → rest, ice, hydration, no training of the affected joint; nutrition: hydrate, limit alcohol (especially beer/spirits), organ meats, sugary drinks; weight loss gradually (rapid loss/fasting can trigger flares — unsourced). Outcome: flare count, urate (if tested), weight, alcohol units.
- These profiles differ from §1/§6 by (a) pre-exercise safety gates (BP, glucose, flare status), (b) disease-specific KPIs (BP, HbA1c, urate, liver enzymes), (c) the isometric block for hypertension and post-meal walks for diabetes.

### Gaps
- STEPS 2019 fact-sheet figures for raised blood glucose %, mean salt intake and alcohol prevalence not retrieved (WHO extranet blocked).
- Mongolia-specific gout (not hyperuricemia) prevalence and the source year behind the 18 %/5 % figures not retrieved.
- Diabetes Prevention Program (58 % risk reduction with 150 min/week + 7 % weight loss) not retrieved this session.
- Exercise effect sizes on LDL/HDL (e.g., Kelley meta-analyses) not retrieved.
- Edwards isometric protocol specifics (4 × 2 min, 3×/week) not confirmed from the paper.

## 11. Teens / students (school-age, exam stress)

### Takeaway
Physical-activity interventions in adolescents produce small-to-moderate improvements in internalizing problems (d ≈ 0.32) and academic achievement (d ≈ 0.37), and more active students report less academic stress and better sleep; WHO's target is 60 min/day with strength/bone work 3×/week.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analytic review of 57 studies (216 effect sizes): PA interventions improved internalizing problems d = 0.316 and academic achievement d = 0.367 — [Spruit et al. 2016, PubMed 27064552](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27064552/)
- Systematic review: 13 studies consistently show higher regular PA associated with lower school-related/academic stress — [University of Granada record](https://produccioncientifica.ugr.es/documentos/672d0f231d259469c25ac101?lang=en); [Psychology, Society & Education review](https://journals.uco.es/psye/article/view/17703)
- Academic stress correlates with anxiety/depression, which correlate negatively with PA and sleep; PA mediates the stress→anxiety link; high PA in high-schoolers improved sleep, lowering anxiety and stress (China, 2025) — [Frontiers in Public Health 2025](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1651883/epub); [PMC8304898](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8304898)
- WHO: 5–17 y average 60 min/day MVPA, muscle- and bone-strengthening 3 days/week — [WHO 2020](https://www.who.int/news/item/25-11-2020-every-move-counts-towards-better-health-says-who)
- PCOS guideline for adolescents mirrors this: ≥60 min/day MVPA + 3×/week strengthening — [PMC10958708](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10958708)

### Inferences
- Student program: short (15–25 min) daily sessions timed as study breaks — bodyweight circuits, dance/cardio, mobility for desk posture, 5-min breathing before study; 3 days include jumping/bodyweight strength for bone; no calorie restriction or weight-loss framing for minors (route body-image concerns to a parent/clinician).
- Avoid: fasting/diet culture content, maximal lifting without supervision, late-night HIIT before exams (sleep), screen-based "challenges" that reward volume over consistency.
- Nutrition: regular breakfast, protein and calcium for growth, hydration; no deficit.
- Outcomes: minutes/day, mood check-in (1–5), sleep hours, perceived stress, fitness tests (push-up, shuttle/step test).
- Differs from adults: no weight-loss goals, bone-loading emphasis, parental consent and age-appropriate language.

### Gaps
- NSCA/ACSM youth resistance-training position statements (safety of supervised RT for children) not retrieved.
- No Mongolia-specific adolescent activity data retrieved (WHO Mongolia GSHS exists but was not found).

## 12. Shift workers, herders/physical labourers, very sedentary beginners with obesity

### Takeaway
For shift workers the evidence is thin but points to walking/aerobic programs improving intermediate NCD risk factors and possibly sleep; for already-active labourers the gap to fill is structured strength, mobility and recovery rather than more volume; for sedentary obese beginners, chair-based and low-impact progressions are effective and well tolerated.

### Cited Findings
- Systematic review of PA-based interventions in shift workers: most used walking/aerobic prescriptions; PA may mitigate intermediate NCD risk factors; little insight into behaviour change — [Bond University record](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/a-systematic-review-of-physical-activity-based-interventions-in-s/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s13142-017-0471-7)
- 2026 systematic review of RCTs of structured exercise on sleep and cognitive outcomes in shift workers — [PMC12920463](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12920463/); Cochrane review of non-drug interventions (bright light, napping, exercise) for sleepiness/sleep problems in night workers — [Cochrane CD010641](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD010641_non-drug-interventions-sleepiness-and-sleep-problems-shift-workers-who-work-nights)
- Evening/pre-bed non-vigorous exercise does not impair and may deepen sleep; vigorous exercise ending ≤1 h before bed can impair it — [Stutz 2018 via BetterSleep](https://bettersleep.com/blog/morning-vs-evening-exercise-for-sleep-what-30-studies-say)
- Chair-based exercise improves upper- and lower-limb function (1,388 participants) — [PMC7920319](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7920319/)
- HIIT and MICT yield the same fat loss in overweight/obese adults (≈2.6 kg) — [MedicalXpress on Wewege 2017](https://m.medicalxpress.com/news/2017-04-moderate-high-intensity-similar-results.html)
- Walking benefit begins ≈4,000 steps/day; each +1,000 steps −15 % mortality — [Banach 2023 via MDLinx](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/just-4-000-steps-a-day-can-lower-your-risk-of-death-says-new-study/4WiaUHi6WS1jRSGnQoXQFb)
- Mongolia: 64 % overweight/obesity, 28.4 % low activity (2019) — [STEPS trends](https://ijph.tums.ac.ir/index.php/ijph/article/download/27939/7987)

### Inferences
- Shift workers: anchor sessions to the shift, not the clock — moderate 20–30 min before a night shift or after waking; gentle yoga/nidra (§5) after a night shift before day-sleep; avoid vigorous work within 1 h of the sleep window; meal timing module (limit large meals at night). KPIs: sleep duration/quality, fatigue score, BP.
- Herders/manual labourers: already meet aerobic volume; the program is 2×/week 20–30 min strength (posterior chain, grip, shoulders), daily 10 min mobility for hips/thoracic/wrists, recovery and sleep, and screening for BP/urate given high alcohol and salt exposure. Avoid adding cardio volume or deficit diets to an already high-expenditure day. KPIs: pain sites, BP, grip strength, mobility tests.
- Sedentary obese beginners: tier 0 = chair-based and wall-supported movements, 10 min/day; tier 1 = standing bodyweight, 15–20 min, 3×/week + steps +500/week from baseline; no jumping or floor-to-stand transitions until tolerated; expect function gains at 4–6 weeks before weight changes; nutrition as §1 but with slower deficit (~300–500 kcal). KPIs: 30-s chair stand, steps, waist, RPE for daily tasks.
- Differences: these three profiles differ by what is missing — sleep timing (shift), strength/mobility/recovery (labourers), tolerance and joint-sparing (obese beginners) — not by the headline goal.

### Gaps
- The "physical activity paradox" literature (occupational PA not conferring the same benefit as leisure PA; Holtermann) could not be retrieved.
- No Mongolian herder-specific health or activity study retrieved.
- Chair-based meta-analysis dose details (sessions/week, weeks) not fetched.

## 13. Event goals: 5k/10k (Couch to 5K), hiking/mountain season, wedding/trip deadline, "summer body" 8–12 weeks, Naadam wrestling-style strength

### Takeaway
Graded run–walk programs like Couch to 5K are the recommended way to start running (and the postnatal return-to-running pathway); 8–12 week deadline programs can realistically deliver ~2–3 kg fat loss from exercise alone and far more with diet, visible strength change, and measurable fitness, but the app must set expectations and build a maintenance phase; wrestling-specific evidence was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Couch to 5K is cited by the Goom/Donnelly/Brockwell guideline as a graded return-to-running pathway — [Athletics NI PDF](https://athleticsni.org/download/files/Returning_to_running_postnatal_guideline_for_medical_health_and_fitness_professionals_managing_this_population.05.pdf)
- HIIT vs MICT in overweight/obese adults: ~2.6 kg fat loss over typical 8–12 week protocols, HIIT in ~40 % less time — [MedicalXpress](https://m.medicalxpress.com/news/2017-04-moderate-high-intensity-similar-results.html); [PMC10048683](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10048683/)
- Exercise-only long-term weight loss is 0–2 kg; diet+exercise beats diet by ~1 kg — [PubMed 19175510](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19175510/)
- Resistance training beginners: near-maximal hypertrophy from 2 sessions/week, 2–3 sets per exercise ~40 % better than 1 set — [Peter Attia MD](https://peterattiamd.com/lowering-the-barrier-to-entry-for-resistance-training/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-017-0762-7/fulltext.html)
- Each 1-MET gain in fitness → 11–17 % lower mortality (a motivational frame for the 5k goal) — [PubMed 38599681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38599681/)
- Weight-loss maintenance needs 200–300 min/week — [IDEA/ACSM](https://www.ideafit.com/acsm-on-weight-loss)

### Inferences
- 5k/10k: 8–9 week run–walk progression 3×/week (C25K structure), 10 % weekly volume cap, 1–2 strength/mobility days (calves, hips); indoor alternative (stairs, treadmill, marching intervals) for winter/pollution days; 10k = +4–6 weeks. KPIs: continuous run minutes, 5k time, resting HR.
- Hiking/mountain season (May–Sept): 8 weeks of step-ups with a loaded backpack, stair climbing 30–45 min 2×/week, single-leg strength and ankle stability, long walk weekly increasing to 2–3 h; KPIs: stair-climb minutes, loaded step-up reps, downhill knee pain.
- Wedding/trip deadline and "summer body" 8–12 weeks: honest promise = 4–8 kg with diet deficit + 3 strength + 2–3 cardio sessions/week, visible posture/tone change; include "after" plan (regain prevention per §1) because deadline goals have the worst long-term adherence (inference). KPIs: weight, waist, photos, strength tests.
- Naadam wrestling-style strength: the goal maps to general strength and power (hinge, squat, carries, grip, neck/trunk isometrics), rotational core, hip mobility and conditioning; 3 strength days + 1–2 conditioning days for 12 weeks. No wrestling-specific trial evidence was found; treat as a culturally framed strength program.
- Avoid: in all deadline programs, extreme deficits, dehydration tactics, daily HIIT (recovery), and marketing that implies spot reduction.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed outcome study of Couch to 5K adherence/completion rates retrieved.
- No evidence on hiking-specific preparation programs or on Mongolian/freestyle wrestling training retrieved.

## 14. Habit building for people who have failed before (behaviour-change science)

### Takeaway
Implementation intentions ("when-where-how" plans) raise goal attainment with a medium effect (d = 0.65) and physical activity specifically with a small effect (≈0.25) that is stronger with reinforcement; autonomous/intrinsic motivation predicts long-term exercise adherence; habits take a median ~66 days (range 18–254) of consistent cue-linked repetition — so the app should default to tiny, cue-anchored, self-chosen actions and plan for ~10 weeks before expecting automaticity.

### Cited Findings
- Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 review: 94 tests, 63 reports; implementation intentions → d = 0.65 on goal attainment — [Silicon Canals summary](https://siliconcanals.com/t-implementation-intentions-when-where-how-goal-achievement/); [The Behavioral Scientist glossary](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/implementation-intentions)
- Meta-analysis of 13 RCTs of implementation intentions for physical activity in adults: effect size 0.25 favouring intervention when the strategy was reinforced; capable of increasing PA practice — [PMC6235272](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6235272/); [McMaster Optimal Aging summary](https://www.mcmasteroptimalaging.org/full-article/HE/meta-analytic-review-effect-implementation-intentions-physical-activity-25322)
- Self-determination theory (Teixeira et al. 2012, IJBNPA): consistent positive relation between autonomous motivation and exercise; identified regulation predicts initial adoption, intrinsic motivation predicts long-term adherence — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/7f329e2f5a7f4f35bdd468a949a85a40); [Chatzisarantis & Hagger meta-analysis PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2003_ChatzHaggerEtAl_MetaAnalysis.pdf); [Frontiers in Psychology 2018](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02141/text)
- Lally et al. 2010 (UCL): median 66 days to automaticity, range 18–254 days; missing one day did not materially derail habit formation — [University of Surrey interview with Lally](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/does-it-really-take-66-days-form-habit-we-asked-expert-dr-pippa-lally); [The Behavioral Scientist analysis](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit)
- Minimum effective training dose is very low (1 set/week for strength, 4 sets/week for growth) — supports a "tiny start" default — [Sci-Sport](https://sci-sport.com/en/resistance-minimum-dose-for-better-health-241/)
- Neck-pain trial: 9 × 7 min/week worked as well as 1 × 60 min — micro-sessions are legitimate — [Copenhagen record](https://www.ucviden.dk/en/publications/influence-of-frequency-and-duration-of-strength-training-for-effe/)

### Inferences
- Onboarding for "failed before": ask what the user values (autonomy/SDT), offer 2–3 program options rather than one prescription, set a tiny default (5–10 min, 2–3×/week), and create an explicit if-then plan ("after morning tea, on the mat by the bed, 7-minute routine"); escalate dose only after 2–3 weeks of ≥80 % completion.
- Build for a 10–12 week habit horizon with a "never miss twice" rule rather than streak-shaming; weekly booster prompts (reinforcement moderated the implementation-intention effect).
- Measure behaviour, not just outcomes: completion rate, days/week, self-reported automaticity (SRBAI-type item), enjoyment rating, autonomous-motivation items at week 0/6/12.
- Differs from goal-specific programs by prioritizing frequency and ease over physiological dose for the first month; the goal-specific dose (§1–13) is layered in only after adherence is established.

### Gaps
- BJ Fogg's Tiny Habits model and Michie's COM-B/Behaviour Change Wheel are widely used frameworks but no peer-reviewed evaluation of them was retrieved this session (search budget exhausted before that query ran).
- Kaushal & Rhodes (6 weeks, 4 sessions/week to form an exercise habit) not retrieved.
